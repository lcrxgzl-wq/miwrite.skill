# Literature Review Rubric

## Core Goal

The literature review tool should synthesize provided literature into a coherent academic review. It should not merely list papers one by one.

## Required Checks

- All cited works come from the user-provided literature.
- No invented author, year, title, DOI, journal, method, or finding.
- The review compares themes, methods, debates, and gaps across studies.
- It identifies tensions and research gaps without overstating novelty.
- Citation style follows the user's requested format.
- The structure matches the user's writing purpose and audience.

## Preferred Output Qualities

- Uses theme-based organization when possible.
- Explains why groups of studies matter to the research topic.
- Distinguishes empirical findings, theoretical claims, and methodological approaches.
- Uses cautious language for claims that are only partially supported.
- Avoids formulaic openings such as "With the rapid development of..."

## Common Failures

- Listing papers one by one with no synthesis across them.
- Using "current research generally agrees" without evidence.
- Inventing missing publication details.
- Turning an annotated bibliography into a full review without adding synthesis.
- Adding irrelevant background knowledge not present in the input.
